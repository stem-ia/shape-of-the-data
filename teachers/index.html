How students save and hand in work

  • Add this graph to My work. Under the graph, the student writes what the graph shows and adds it. My work keeps the graph, its settings, their shape choice and their sentence.
  • The checklist. My work shows five "I did this" lines, one per outcome, and which graph is the evidence for each.
  • Hand it in. "Save as a picture" makes one image of everything. "Print or save as PDF" does the same on paper or as a PDF. The student uploads the file to your Schoology assignment.
  • A different device next class. My work saves on the device as they go. If they will not get the same device back, they press "Save a work file" at the end of class and "Open a work file" next time.
  • Shared devices. "Clear this device" (two taps) wipes the last student's graph settings and My work.

Nothing is sent anywhere. There are no logins, and work only leaves the device when a student saves a file. The Name box goes on the saved file only and is not stored.

S2 Interval sizeTwo or more graphs of the same data at different interval sizesThey say what changed in the shape and which size tells the clearest story
S3 Equal-interval histogramsAny saved graph, and the hand-drawn histogram from the worksheet or the Data BankEqual intervals, sensible start, labelled axes
S4 Compare two samplesA Side by side or On top graphA comparison of shape and centre, not only "bigger"
S5 Mean, median or modeA graph with a measure showing, plus their sentenceA choice of measure and a reason tied to the shape

Marking Data Bank histograms

Add ?key to the Data Bank link to turn on the teacher key: bank/?key. Pick the student's column and interval width. The planner then shows the count in every interval, a sketch of the shape, and the mean, median, most common value and range. It works for any column, filter and width.

There is no button for the key on the student page. A student who has this link can see it, so share it with staff only.

Running it

  • Devices: Chromebooks and iPads, in any current browser. No install.
  • Old version showing? Refresh the page.
  • The lab remembers each topic's graph, step and prediction on that device, so a returning student carries on where they stopped.
  • Tiny bars: at very small interval sizes the bars are narrow. Students can still read them; for tapping, a bigger interval is easier.
